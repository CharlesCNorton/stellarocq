\title{Stellarocq: Machine-Checked Certificates for Stellarator Equilibria}
\author{Charles C. Norton\\\normalsize\texttt{cnorton2@binghamton.edu}}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Stellarator design rests on ideal-MHD equilibria computed by VMEC and its
descendants, whose output is a file of floating-point Fourier coefficients.
Stellarocq turns questions about such a file into machine-checked theorems
about the magnetic field it defines. The field is reconstructed from the exact
numbers of a VMEC++ output file by VMEC's own half-grid rule. Force balance,
harmonics, flux-surface integrals, the Mercier criterion, quasisymmetry and
free-boundary balance are expressions over that reconstruction, and each claim
about them is decided by a checker extracted from a Rocq proof that its verdict
implies the claim. Testing VMEC++ against an invariance the development proves
exposed an orientation test that the poloidal rotation VMEC applies to
asymmetric boundaries defeats, which VMEC++ now tests with an invariant. On
VMEC++ output the certificates measure the error of VMEC's half-grid rule on
manufactured problems to twelve digits, certify a resonant harmonic that radial
refinement does not remove, and bound the free-boundary pressure jump for every
vacuum pressure between the answers of NESTOR and BIEST.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Variational Moments Equilibrium Code (VMEC)~\cite{hirshman1983,hirshman1986}
computes three-dimensional ideal-MHD equilibria with nested flux surfaces by
minimizing the plasma energy over Fourier representations of the surfaces. It
and its reimplementation VMEC++~\cite{schilling2025,vmecpp} sit at the centre of
stellarator design and optimization. A run ends with a \emph{wout} file: Fourier
coefficients of the surfaces on a radial grid, profiles, and derived quantities
such as the Mercier stability terms, all in binary64 floating point.

What such a file establishes about the field it describes is usually a matter of
convergence metrics internal to the solver and of diagnostics recomputed in
floating point. Stellarocq states it as a theorem. The object of every statement
is the field reconstructed from the exact numbers in the file by the rule the
solver itself uses. A claim about that field is decided by a checking function
written in Rocq~\cite{rocq}, proven to imply the claim when it returns
\code{true}, and extracted to OCaml~\cite{letouzey2003}. Real-number semantics
and interval arithmetic come from Coquelicot~\cite{coquelicot},
Flocq~\cite{flocq} and CoqInterval~\cite{melquiond2008}.

Where floating point estimates, a certificate decides. An invariance test whose
expected answer the development fixes exposed an orientation test that VMEC's
own relabelling of an asymmetric boundary defeats; it was reported to VMEC++ and
to educational\_VMEC, and VMEC++ now tests an invariant. The error of VMEC's
half-grid rule, collocated on manufactured problems, is known to twelve digits,
with no solver tolerance in it, and falls as $h^2$ in three dimensions, without
stellarator symmetry and at five times the pressure. On a rippled tokamak the
harmonic of the force that a nested-surface field cannot balance at a rational
surface is bounded away from zero at every state within a relative $10^{-12}$ of
the converged one, and the
free-boundary pressure jump of a VMEC++ equilibrium is enclosed for every vacuum
field between the answers of two independent solvers.

Section~\ref{sec:object} states the certified object and the theorem that
identifies its residual with the ideal-MHD force, Section~\ref{sec:certs} the
certificate forms and their soundness theorems, and Section~\ref{sec:results}
what they establish on VMEC++ output. The development is available at
\link{https://github.com/CharlesCNorton/stellarocq}.

\section{The certified object}\label{sec:object}

\paragraph{Reconstruction.}
VMEC keeps the surface coefficients of $R$ and $Z$ on the full radial grid
$s_j = jh$ and the stream function $\lambda$, the rotational transform $\iota$
and every field quantity on the half grid $s_{j+1/2}$. Stellarocq's
\code{Physics.v} writes the rule that connects them. On a half point $s_h$
between nodes $a$ and $b$, a coefficient of even poloidal mode number is the
mean of its two node values and one of odd mode number is averaged with the
$\sqrt{s}$ weight VMEC folds into its Jacobian,
\begin{equation}
c_k(s_h) = {\textstyle\frac12}\bigl(c_k(a)+c_k(b)\bigr)\ \ (m_k \mbox{ even}),\qquad
c_k(s_h) = {\textstyle\frac12}\sqrt{s_h}\,\Bigl(\frac{c_k(a)}{\sqrt{s_a}}+\frac{c_k(b)}{\sqrt{s_b}}\Bigr)\ \ (m_k \mbox{ odd}),
\end{equation}
with radial derivatives the matching difference quotients (for odd $m_k$ plus
the derivative of the $\sqrt{s}$ factor), and the angular derivatives exact
derivatives of the series $R=\sum_k c_k\cos(m_k u-n_k v)$,
$Z=\sum_k z_k\sin(m_k u-n_k v)$. The half-grid $\lambda$ and $\iota$ are the
file's own, $\Phi'$ is constant, and the pressure is the input's profile. For
statements at an arbitrary radius each coefficient of $R$ and $Z$ is the cubic
Hermite interpolant of its half-point values and slopes, which agrees with the
half-grid rule at the half points; those of $\lambda$ follow the same parity
rule linearly between half points, and $\iota$ is linear there.

\paragraph{Field and residual.}
With $\sqg = R(R_uZ_s-R_sZ_u)$, the field is
$B^u=\Phi'(\iota-\lambda_v)/\sqg$, $B^v=\Phi'(1+\lambda_u)/\sqg$, its covariant
components $B_i=g_{iu}B^u+g_{iv}B^v$, and $\mu_0\sqg J^s=\partial_uB_v-\partial_vB_u$.
The residual of $\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}=\nabla p$ has the components
\begin{equation}
r_u = -\mu_0\sqg J^s B^v,\qquad r_v = \mu_0\sqg J^s B^u \qquad\mbox{(at the outer half point)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
r_s = (\partial_vB_s-\partial_sB_v)B^v-(\partial_sB_u-\partial_uB_s)B^u-\mu_0\,dp/ds \qquad\mbox{(at the node)},
\end{equation}
where at the node radial derivatives are centred differences of half-grid values
and values are their averages, as in VMEC. These are \code{expr} trees, which
\code{Physics.v} states without proofs, and the development proves what they
compute. Read at a free radius, with every radial derivative that of the Hermite
reconstruction, they form the continuum residual, whose three outputs are the
covariant components along $\mathbf{e}_s$, $\mathbf{e}_u$ and $\mathbf{e}_v$ of
$\mu_0(\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}-\nabla p)$ of the reconstructed field. The
theorem quantifies over the Cartesian field instead of constructing it: for any
$\mathbf{G}$ and $P$ differentiable at the point that equal the reconstructed
field and pressure along the three coordinate lines through it
(\code{represents}), the outputs are $\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{e}_i$ with
$\mathbf{F}=(\nabla\times\mathbf{G})\times\mathbf{G}-\mu_0\nabla P$. The
derivative of $\mathbf{G}$ is fixed by its values along three independent lines,
so no inverse of the coordinate map enters. Abridged, with the hypotheses that
give the certificate's slots their values elided:
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
  Theorem continuum_force ... :
    let r := residual exps (PConfig false prof RRadial) modes in
    well_formed wm (r_binds r) = true -> ... ->
    filterdiff G1 (locally (emb FR FZ s0 u0 v0)) (lin3 a11 a12 a13) -> ... ->
    represents G1 G2 G3 P (fun t => emb FR FZ t u0 v0) ... p s0 -> ... ->
    let w1 := a32 - a23 in let w2 := a13 - a31 in let w3 := a21 - a12 in
    let F1 := (w2 * G03 - w3 * G02) - mu0r * q1 in ...
    xeval E (r_s r) = Xreal (dot3 F1 F2 F3 (FRs s0 u0 v0 * c) (FRs s0 u0 v0 * sn)
                                   (FZs s0 u0 v0)) /\ ...
\end{verbatim}}
\noindent\code{continuum\_force\_asym} is the same statement for a state without
stellarator symmetry, and both rest on \code{Force.force\_law}, the identity at
one point. Read at a node, the angular outputs are those formulas at the outer
half point and the radial output is VMEC's combination of the fields at the two
half points, each field that of the series with VMEC's half-grid values and
slopes (\code{node\_residual}, \code{node\_residual\_asym}).

\paragraph{Identities of the reconstruction.}
Properties a floating-point code can only sample are theorems here.
The reconstructed field is exactly solenoidal (\code{divergence\_free}); the
$(0,0)$ coefficient of $\lambda$ drives nothing (\code{lambda\_gauge}), which
exhibits the poloidal relabelling as an exact kernel direction of the
linearized problem; the residual has no component along the field
(\code{residual\_along\_field}); and the Euler--Lagrange expressions of
VMEC's energy $W=\int(B^2/2-\mu_0p)\sqg$ at fixed $\lambda$, $\iota$, flux and
pressure are $-\sqg$ times the cylindrical components of the force
(\code{energy\_gradient\_is\_force}), so that the forces the solver drives to
zero are the ideal-MHD force. \code{Symmetry.v} proves that a quasisymmetric
field makes the two-term ratio $(\mathbf{B}\times\nabla s\cdot\nabla B)/(\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla B)$
a flux function and the triple product $\nabla s\cdot(\nabla B\times\nabla(\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla B))$
vanish, which is the direction a certificate refuting quasisymmetry rests on.
Physical assumptions, such as second-order consistency of the discretization,
are propositions in \code{Hypotheses.v} carried as explicit hypotheses of the
theorems that use them, never as axioms.

\section{Certificates and their soundness}\label{sec:certs}

A certificate is a text file that a Python generator in \code{gen/} writes from a
wout file. It carries the file's numbers and the bounds it claims, and the
extracted checker recomputes every enclosure from those numbers, so nothing the
generator computes is trusted.

\paragraph{Expressions and intervals.}
\code{Expr.v} gives \code{expr} two meanings: a value over extended reals, in
which division by zero and the square root of a negative number give
\code{Xnan}, and a value over CoqInterval's floating-point intervals.
\code{ieval\_correct} states that the interval contains the extended-real value,
so a bounded output interval proves both that the value is a real number and
that it lies in the bounds. The embedding is deep, so one tree carries both
meanings and \code{Deriv.v} differentiates it symbolically. Subexpressions shared
between the residual components are bound once to environment slots by a
builder that only grows, so a binding made at any stage of the reconstruction is
among its final ones, and \code{iextend\_correct} states that extending both
environments by the same bindings preserves containment.

\paragraph{Points.}
A point certificate carries, for every environment slot, the integer mantissa
and power-of-two exponent of a double of the wout file, so no rounding happens
between the file and the certificate, together with claimed bounds $N2^q$ on the
three residual components; \code{check\_cert\_correct} states that a passing
check bounds every component at every point.

\paragraph{Cells and coverings.}
\code{Deriv.v} proves the chain rule for its derivatives in CoqInterval's
extended-real sense. A cell certificate bounds a component over a rectangle of
two varied slots (two angles, or an angle and the radius) by the mean-value
combination of its centre value and interval enclosures of its derivatives over
the rectangle (\code{check\_ccert\_correct}). Reversed, the same data proves a
component bounded away from zero, so that no field of the reconstructed form
balances in the cell. That the cells of a certificate over the radius and the
angles cover the rectangle they span is not assumed from the generator that
wrote them: the driver decides it slab by slab of the other slot, each slab by
the chain of integer inequalities of \code{Cover.v}, and a certificate whose
cells leave a gap or a hole is rejected like one whose bounds are too small.
The cells of an integral have to partition its range, each tile exactly once,
which the driver checks in integers before it sums them.

\paragraph{Harmonics and integrals.}
A spectral solution balances forces mode by mode over the modes it retains, so
the quantity it makes small is the discrete Fourier harmonic of the residual at
a retained mode, a finite sum $\sum_i r(p_i)\cos(mu_i-nv_i)$ over a uniform
grid of angles that \code{Project.v} encloses (\code{harm\_encloses}).
\code{TrigPoly.v} and \code{Harmonic.v} integrate exactly the components whose
trigonometric degree lies below the point counts of an equispaced grid, and
\code{Quad.v} encloses other angular integrals cell by cell. \code{Mercier.v}
assembles DShear, DCurr, DWell, DGeod and DMerc as VMEC's \code{mercier.f90}
defines them~\cite{mercier1960,bauer1984} from enclosed integrals, inside the
extracted code, so that a verdict is a theorem about the criterion rather than
about its ingredients. DGeod is the Cauchy--Schwarz defect of three surface
integrals and is proven never positive, whatever their enclosures
(\code{dgeod\_nonpositive}).

\paragraph{Boxes of states.}
In \code{Box.v} every input slot carries a half-width, so one interval
evaluation covers every field whose coefficients lie in a box. A floor certified
over a box holds for every state in it, not only for the one at which a solver
stopped.

\paragraph{Discrete equilibria.}
\code{Newton.v} proves that a passing interval Newton test in Krawczyk
form~\cite{krawczyk1969,moore2009} on any system of expressions gives exactly one
zero in its box: the map $x-AF(x)$ contracts the box into itself, an
approximate inverse of $A$ makes its fixed point a zero of $F$, and the
contraction makes that zero unique. \code{Colloc.v} collocates the residual of
\code{Physics.v} over a band of surfaces as such a system, with the surface
coefficients as unknowns:
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
  Theorem colloc_correct : forall prec n centre r A B KN Kq MN Mq pts,
    colloc_check prec n centre r A B KN Kq MN Mq pts = true ->
    exists x : list R,
      in_box (colloc_system prec n centre r A B KN Kq MN Mq pts) x /\
      (forall p, In p pts -> xeval (xextend (local_env n centre x p)
                   (r_binds (local_res p))) (local_out p) = Xreal 0) /\
      (forall y, in_box (colloc_system prec n centre r A B KN Kq MN Mq pts) y ->
         (forall p, In p pts -> xeval (xextend (local_env n centre y p)
                      (r_binds (local_res p))) (local_out p) = Xreal 0) ->
         y = x).
\end{verbatim}}
\noindent\code{Wide.v} evaluates the system's outputs at the centre of the box in
floating point of any precision.

\paragraph{Trust base.}
The kernel checks the proofs; what runs is the OCaml extraction of the checking
functions, linked with an unverified driver that parses certificates into the
extracted data types and prints verdicts. The trusted components are therefore
the Rocq kernel and extraction, the OCaml toolchain, the implementation of
Rocq's primitive 63-bit integers and binary64 floats, the driver's parser and
its covering and partition checks, and
the correspondence between a certificate's dyadic numbers and the doubles of
its wout file, which a separate tool, \code{gen/verify\_cert.py}, compares. The
classical constructions behind Rocq's real numbers enter the extraction only
through the specifications, and \code{gen/patch\_extract.py} replaces them by
values that fail when consulted. For the soundness theorems above,
\code{Print Assumptions} reports the two axioms of the standard library's
classical Dedekind reals, excluded middle, functional extensionality and the
specifications of the primitive operations. The build pins Rocq 9.1.1,
CoqInterval 4.11.4, Flocq 4.2.2, Coquelicot 3.4.5 and OCaml 4.14.2.

\paragraph{An orientation test that is not gauge invariant.}
Four invariance tests of VMEC++ have expected answers that the development
fixes: an axisymmetric input solved with and without toroidal modes
(every toroidal derivative of an axisymmetric reconstruction is the zero
expression), a stellarator-symmetric input solved through the symmetric and the
asymmetric code paths (a series whose coefficients vanish contributes nothing),
a restart with a shifted $(0,0)$ coefficient of $\lambda$ (\code{lambda\_gauge}),
and a five-period stellarator solved over one period and over the torus (the
reconstruction reads each mode by its absolute toroidal number). The
second exposed an orientation test that is not invariant under the poloidal
rotation VMEC applies to an asymmetric boundary: the test multiplies two
$m=1$ coefficients whose product the rotation can drive to zero, where the
invariant is the $m=1$ determinant $r^{cc}z^{sc}-r^{sc}z^{cc}$. Relabelling the
\code{up\_down\_asym\_current} boundary by $\theta\to\pi-\theta$ sends
educational\_VMEC to its iteration cap at $f_{sqr}=2.07\times10^{-2}$ instead of
converging to $5.07\times10^{-12}$. It was reported as
vmecpp\#788~\cite{issue788}, after which VMEC++ tests the
determinant~\cite{pr789}, and as educational\_VMEC\#27~\cite{issue27}.

\paragraph{Discrete equilibria and the order of the scheme.}
One, two and three surfaces of the Solov'ev equilibrium~\cite{soloviev1968}
each have exactly one zero of the collocated residual in a box shaped to the
coefficients, of radius $5.7\times10^{-10}$ to $6.3\times10^{-9}$, around a
centre that Newton's method moved $2.2\times10^{-6}$ to $3.5\times10^{-6}$
from the file's coefficients; with five surfaces the test does not pass.
\code{HalfGrid.v} proves the node rule second-order consistent with the
residual of the continuously reconstructed field (\code{node\_consistent}) and
derives the second-order bound that \code{discretization\_is\_consistent}
states from consistency and a stability hypothesis (\code{lax\_second\_order});
near the axis, where the innermost interval is read linearly, the rule is
proven first order in the radial derivatives. Manufactured
solutions~\cite{roache2002} measure the order directly. A manufactured mapping
on the annulus $1/4<s<1$ is not an equilibrium; its continuum residual at the
collocation points is a source $f$, and the zero $x_h$ of $r-f$ over the
interior nodes, with $\lambda$ given so that the problem sits on the quotient by
the poloidal gauge, is the discrete solution. On systems of up to 423 unknowns,
the interval Newton test puts exactly one zero in a box $5\times10^{-18}$ wide
per unknown around a centre
refined on outputs read at 128 bits, so the error $\max|x_h-x^*|$ is known to
twelve digits (Table~\ref{tab:results}). It falls by 3.81, 3.92 and 3.97 per
doubling for a three-dimensional mapping and by 3.73 and 3.87 for one without
stellarator symmetry (Figure~\ref{fig:conv}a), and it is unchanged at five times
the pressure, which enters the radial force only as a flux function the rule and
its source share.

\paragraph{A forced current sheet.}
On VMEC++'s circular tokamak ($R_0=6$, $a=2$) with $\iota=0.9-0.64s$, which is
$1/2$ at $s=0.625$, and a $(1,1)$ boundary ripple of amplitude $0.03$, toroidal
coupling drives a $(2,1)$ harmonic of the radial force on the rational surface.
Its certified value, the rectangle rule for the integral of $r_s\cos(2u-v)$ on
a $64\times32$ grid of angles, enclosed by \code{dharm\_correct}, stays between
$6.2\times10^{-5}$ and $9.1\times10^{-5}$ from 65 to 257 surfaces and is linear
in the ripple (Figure~\ref{fig:conv}c); the enclosure at 65 surfaces holds for
every state within a relative $10^{-12}$ of the converged coefficients. On the
same surface the axisymmetric harmonics $(2,0)$ and $(1,0)$ fall by 3.8 to 5.3
per doubling, the order of the scheme, while the $(3,1)$ sideband falls by only
1.2 to 1.3 (Figure~\ref{fig:conv}b), and the same ripple on a transform profile
with no low-order rational surface nearby leaves nothing behind. This is the
obstruction a nested-surface field meets at a rational
surface~\cite{grad1967,hahm1985}, whose ideal response is a current sheet. In
SPEC~\cite{hudson2012}, two Beltrami volumes separated by an ideal interface at
the rational surface carry the $(2,1)$ sheet as a jump of $1.47\times10^{-5}$ in
the covariant poloidal field at ripple $0.03$, as in the equilibria
of~\cite{loizu2015}, and with the rational surface inside a relaxed volume
SPEC's field-line tracing finds a $(2,1)$ island whose width grows from $0.107$
to $0.170$ in the volume's radial coordinate as the ripple goes from $0.0075$ to
$0.03$.

\paragraph{Free-boundary balance.}
At a free-boundary equilibrium $p+B^2/2$ is continuous across the plasma
boundary, where outside it is the pressure of the full vacuum field. VMEC++
computes that field with NESTOR~\cite{merkel1986,hirshman1986}; BIEST's
virtual-casing field~\cite{malhotra2019,malhotra2020} gives it independently. The
certificate sweeps the vacuum pressure over the segment between the two answers,
$P_{\rm vac}=P_{\rm mid}+t\,\delta P$ with $t\in[-1,1]$, and the plasma
side is VMEC's extrapolation of the two outermost half points to $s=1$. On the
CTH-like test case, NESTOR's jump where it is largest is certified at least
$7.556\times10^{-4}$ at 25 surfaces, and the jump at every point of NESTOR's
grid at most $7.560\times10^{-4}$ for every $t$. At 49, 97, 193 and 385
surfaces each such ceiling lies below the floor of the resolution before it,
down to $[4.9648,4.9672]\times10^{-4}$ at 385, so the certified jump falls under
radial refinement. Covered over the whole boundary by $512\times128$ cells per field
period, with the vacuum side the trigonometric interpolant of each answer's
grid, it is at most $8.5\times10^{-3}$ at 25 surfaces and $1.05\times10^{-2}$
at 385: between grid points the bound is set by that interpolant, whose angular
resolution radial refinement leaves as it is.

\paragraph{Quasisymmetry.}
A field is quasisymmetric exactly when the two-term ratio is a flux
function~\cite{helander2008,helander2014}. For a quasisymmetric field every
discrete harmonic of the defect of the claim ``the ratio equals $F_0$'' is one
number times the matching harmonic of its second term, so two harmonic ratios
with disjoint enclosures refute it:
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
  Theorem two_term_refuted :
    forall c pts sine1 m1 n1 sine2 m2 n2 (a1 b1 c1 d1 a2 b2 c2 d2 : R),
    (a1 <= harm_r c r_s sine1 m1 n1 pts <= b1) ->
    (c1 <= harm_r c r_v sine1 m1 n1 pts <= d1) -> (0 < c1 \/ d1 < 0) ->
    (a2 <= harm_r c r_s sine2 m2 n2 pts <= b2) ->
    (c2 <= harm_r c r_v sine2 m2 n2 pts <= d2) -> (0 < c2 \/ d2 < 0) ->
    (snd (ratio_box a1 b1 c1 d1) < fst (ratio_box a2 b2 c2 d2) \/
     snd (ratio_box a2 b2 c2 d2) < fst (ratio_box a1 b1 c1 d1)) ->
    forall lam, ~ two_term_constant c lam pts.
\end{verbatim}}
\noindent On \code{wout\_nfp4\_QH\_ns50} the certified ratios span 1.7 at mid
radius, where the axisymmetric Solov'ev equilibrium, quasisymmetric by
construction, spans $2\times10^{-4}$ from the reconstruction's own residual; the
span is unchanged from 50 to 162 modes. The field of that file is not
quasisymmetric on any surface tested.

\paragraph{Low resolution.}
The certified residual of \code{li383\_low\_res} does not fall from 16 to 121
surfaces and falls more than twentyfold from 25 to 98 modes: what a converged
run leaves at this resolution is mode truncation, which the solver's convergence
measure, taken over the retained modes, does not see. At $s=0.125$ of
\code{up\_down\_asym} on sixteen surfaces the certified Mercier current term is
$-3.7\times10^{-3}$ where the file's difference quotients give
$-6.1\times10^{-4}$, reversing the sign of DMerc; from 33 to 257 surfaces both
tend to zero with the file's sign. At sixteen surfaces the sign of DMerc
depends on how the radial derivatives are taken.

\paragraph{Use in VMEC++.}
The certified values enter a branch of VMEC++~\cite{branch} as regression tests
whose tolerances are the enclosures: the manufactured-solution errors, the
resonant floor with the fall of the axisymmetric harmonics, and the
free-boundary jump, which reads NESTOR's boundary vacuum field through an option
proposed upstream~\cite{pr903}. Tests that follow from theorems here are part
of VMEC++: a finite difference of its energy equals its unpreconditioned forces
contracted with the perturbation, the discrete form of
\code{energy\_gradient\_is\_force}~\cite{pr905}, and three of the invariances
above hold~\cite{pr875}. Tests that DGeod is never positive and that the energy
Hessian is symmetric are proposed~\cite{pr904}.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Every theorem here is about the reconstruction of Section~\ref{sec:object},
whose radial interpolation is a choice: a certified value at a free radius is
that field's, and at coarse resolution it can differ from what the solver's
difference quotients give, as the Mercier current term of \code{up\_down\_asym}
does. The collocated system is the half-grid residual at collocation points,
not VMEC's own discrete equations, which come from the variation of the energy
with spectral condensation and a constraint on the $m=1$ modes; its errors
measure the half-grid rule. Second-order convergence of the rule rests on a
stability hypothesis, which the manufactured solutions measure and the
development does not prove. The resonant harmonic is certified over a box a
relative $10^{-12}$ wide, and the bound of the free-boundary covering is set by
the angular interpolation of the vacuum answers.

\section{Related work}

Rigorous numerics proves statements about dynamical systems and partial
differential equations with interval arithmetic: the existence of the Lorenz
attractor~\cite{tucker1999}, the CAPD library for ordinary differential
equations~\cite{capd2021}, KAM theory applied by computer~\cite{figueras2017},
blow-up of the Boussinesq and axisymmetric Euler equations~\cite{chen2023}, and
elliptic and parabolic problems~\cite{nakao2019}. These proofs run in libraries
outside a proof assistant. Inside one, verified ODE solvers up to the Lorenz
attractor~\cite{immler2012,immler2018}, the discretization and floating-point
program of the one-dimensional wave equation~\cite{boldo2013}, the Lax--Milgram
theorem~\cite{boldo2017} and the certificates of the Kepler
conjecture~\cite{hales2017} come closest, and high-energy physics is being
formalized in Lean~\cite{toobysmith2025}. Stellarator equilibrium codes are
verified by convergence studies, manufactured solutions and comparison between
codes, VMEC against SPEC~\cite{hudson2012,loizu2015} and against
DESC~\cite{dudt2020,panici2023}. We know of no prior work that states the output
of a stellarator equilibrium code as a machine-checked theorem about the field
it defines.

\section{Conclusion}

Stellarocq gives the output of an equilibrium code a meaning a proof assistant
can check: bounds, harmonics, stability terms, quasisymmetry verdicts and the
existence of discrete solutions, each a theorem about the field reconstructed
from the file's exact numbers. Its verdicts enter VMEC++ as regression tests
whose tolerances are the certified enclosures.

\paragraph{Availability.}
The development, the certificate generators and the script that writes
Figure~\ref{fig:conv} are at \link{https://github.com/CharlesCNorton/stellarocq},
commit \code{1bea7d5}; \code{make all} builds the proofs and the checker,
\code{make audit} prints the assumptions of every theorem named here, and the release
\code{checker-1bea7d5} carries the same checker linked statically for x86\_64
Linux. The VMEC++ tests that carry the certified values are on the branch
of~\cite{branch}.
