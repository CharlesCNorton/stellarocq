\title{Stellarocq: Machine-Checked Certificates for Stellarator Equilibria}
\author{Charles C. Norton\\\normalsize\texttt{cnorton2@binghamton.edu}}

\begin{abstract}
Stellarator design rests on ideal-MHD equilibria computed by VMEC and its
descendants, whose output is a file of floating-point Fourier coefficients.
Stellarocq is a Rocq development that turns questions about such a file into
theorems about the magnetic field it defines. The field is reconstructed from
the exact numbers in a VMEC++ output file by VMEC's own half-grid rule, force
balance, harmonics, flux-surface integrals, the Mercier criterion, quasisymmetry
and free-boundary balance are expressions over that reconstruction, and each
claim is decided by a checker extracted from a proof that its verdict implies
the claim. On VMEC++ output the certificates attribute a converged residual to
mode truncation, expose an orientation test that is not invariant under the
poloidal rotation VMEC applies to asymmetric boundaries, measure the error of
VMEC's radial scheme on manufactured problems to twelve digits, certify a
resonant harmonic that radial refinement does not remove, and bound the
free-boundary pressure jump for every vacuum pressure between the answers of
NESTOR and BIEST.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Variational Moments Equilibrium Code (VMEC)~\cite{hirshman1983,hirshman1986}
computes three-dimensional ideal-MHD equilibria with nested flux surfaces by
minimizing the plasma energy over Fourier representations of the surfaces. It
and its reimplementation VMEC++~\cite{schilling2025,vmecpp} sit at the centre of
stellarator design and optimization. A run ends with a \emph{wout} file: Fourier
coefficients of the surfaces on a radial grid, profiles, and derived quantities
such as the Mercier stability terms, all in binary64 floating point.

What such a file establishes about the field it describes is usually a matter of
convergence metrics internal to the solver and of diagnostics recomputed in
floating point. Stellarocq states it as a theorem. The object of every statement
is the field reconstructed from the exact numbers in the file by the rule the
solver itself uses. A claim about that field (a bound on the force residual over
a region, the enclosure of a harmonic, the sign of a Mercier term, the existence
of a discrete equilibrium in a box) is decided by a checking function written in
Rocq~\cite{rocq}, proven to imply the claim when it returns \code{true}, and
extracted to OCaml~\cite{letouzey2003} to run at the scale a three-dimensional
equilibrium needs. Real-number semantics and interval arithmetic come from
Coquelicot~\cite{coquelicot}, Flocq~\cite{flocq} and CoqInterval~\cite{melquiond2008}.

Section~\ref{sec:object} states the certified object, Section~\ref{sec:certs}
the certificate forms and their soundness theorems, and
Section~\ref{sec:results} what they establish on VMEC++ output. The part of
the development described here is 29 files and about 22{,}100 lines of Rocq; it
is available at \url{https://github.com/CharlesCNorton/stellarocq}.

\section{The certified object}\label{sec:object}

\paragraph{Reconstruction.}
VMEC keeps the surface coefficients of $R$ and $Z$ on the full radial grid
$s_j = jh$ and the stream function $\lambda$, the rotational transform $\iota$
and every field quantity on the half grid $s_{j+1/2}$. Stellarocq's
\code{Physics.v} writes the rule that connects them. On a half point $s_h$
between nodes $a$ and $b$, a coefficient of even poloidal mode number is the
mean of its two node values and one of odd mode number is averaged with the
$\sqrt{s}$ weight VMEC folds into its Jacobian,
\begin{equation}
c_k(s_h) = \tfrac12\bigl(c_k(a)+c_k(b)\bigr)\ \ (m_k \text{ even}),\qquad
c_k(s_h) = \tfrac12\sqrt{s_h}\,\Bigl(\frac{c_k(a)}{\sqrt{s_a}}+\frac{c_k(b)}{\sqrt{s_b}}\Bigr)\ \ (m_k \text{ odd}),
\end{equation}
with radial derivatives the matching difference quotients (for odd $m_k$ plus
the derivative of the $\sqrt{s}$ factor), and the angular derivatives exact
derivatives of the series $R=\sum_k c_k\cos(m_k u-n_k v)$,
$Z=\sum_k z_k\sin(m_k u-n_k v)$. The half-grid $\lambda$ and $\iota$ are the
file's own, $\Phi'$ is constant, and the pressure is the input's profile. For
statements at an arbitrary radius the half-point values and slopes are joined by
a cubic Hermite interpolant, which agrees with the half-grid rule at the half
points.

\paragraph{Field and residual.}
With $\sqg = R(R_uZ_s-R_sZ_u)$, the field is
$B^u=\Phi'(\iota-\lambda_v)/\sqg$, $B^v=\Phi'(1+\lambda_u)/\sqg$, its covariant
components $B_i=g_{iu}B^u+g_{iv}B^v$, and $\mu_0\sqg J^s=\partial_uB_v-\partial_vB_u$.
The residual of $\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}=\nabla p$ has the components
\begin{align}
r_u &= -\mu_0\sqg J^s B^v,\qquad r_v = \mu_0\sqg J^s B^u \quad\text{(at the outer half point)},\\
r_s &= (\partial_vB_s-\partial_sB_v)B^v-(\partial_sB_u-\partial_uB_s)B^u-\mu_0\,dp/ds \quad\text{(at the node)},
\end{align}
where at the node radial derivatives are centred differences of half-grid values
and values are their averages, as in VMEC. These are \code{expr} trees, which
\code{Physics.v} states without proofs, and the development proves what they
compute. Read at a free radius, with every radial derivative that of the Hermite
reconstruction, they form the continuum residual, whose three outputs are the
covariant components along $\mathbf{e}_s$, $\mathbf{e}_u$ and $\mathbf{e}_v$ of
$\mu_0(\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}-\nabla p)$ of the reconstructed field, with
$\mu_0\mathbf{J}$ the curl of that field computed in Cartesian coordinates
(\code{continuum\_force}, and \code{continuum\_force\_asym} for a state without
stellarator symmetry, from \code{Force.force\_law}). Read at a node, the angular
outputs are those formulas at the outer half point and the radial output is
VMEC's combination of the fields at the two half points, each field that of the
series with VMEC's half-grid values and slopes (\code{node\_residual},
\code{node\_residual\_asym}).

\paragraph{Identities of the reconstruction.}
Properties a floating-point code can only sample are theorems here.
The reconstructed field is exactly solenoidal (\code{divergence\_free}); the
$(0,0)$ coefficient of $\lambda$ drives nothing (\code{lambda\_gauge}), which
exhibits the poloidal relabelling as an exact kernel direction of the
linearized problem; the residual has no component along the field
(\code{residual\_along\_field}); and the Euler--Lagrange expressions of
VMEC's energy $W=\int(B^2/2-\mu_0p)\sqg$ at fixed $\lambda$, $\iota$, flux and
pressure are $-\sqg$ times the cylindrical components of the force
(\code{energy\_gradient\_is\_force}), so that the forces the solver drives to
zero are the ideal-MHD force. \code{Symmetry.v} proves that a quasisymmetric
field makes the two-term ratio $(\mathbf{B}\times\nabla s\cdot\nabla B)/(\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla B)$
a flux function and the triple product $\nabla s\cdot(\nabla B\times\nabla(\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla B))$
vanish, which is the direction a certificate refuting quasisymmetry rests on.
Physical assumptions, such as second-order consistency of the discretization,
are propositions in \code{Hypotheses.v} carried as explicit hypotheses of the
theorems that use them, never as axioms.

\section{Certificates and their soundness}\label{sec:certs}

A certificate is a text file that a Python generator in \code{gen/} writes from a
wout file. It carries the file's numbers and the bounds it claims, and the
extracted checker recomputes every enclosure from those numbers, so nothing the
generator computes is trusted.

\paragraph{Expressions and intervals.}
\code{Expr.v} gives \code{expr} two meanings: a value over extended reals, in
which division by zero and the square root of a negative number give
\code{Xnan}, and a value over CoqInterval's floating-point intervals.
\code{ieval\_correct} states that the interval contains the extended-real value.
A bounded output interval therefore proves both that the value is a real number
and that it lies in the bounds. Subexpressions shared between the residual
components are bound once to environment slots, and \code{iextend\_correct}
states that extending both environments by the same bindings preserves
containment.

\paragraph{Points.}
A point certificate carries, for every environment slot, the integer mantissa
and power-of-two exponent of a double of the wout file, so no rounding happens
between the file and the certificate, together with claimed bounds $N2^q$ on the
three residual components. Its soundness theorem reads:
\begin{lstlisting}
Definition point_sound (c : cert) (p : cpoint) : Prop :=
  let r3 := residual (pt_es p) (c_cfg c) (c_modes c) in
  let env := point_env c p in
  (exists x, xeval env (r_s r3) = Xreal x /\
             Rabs x <= IZR (c_NS c) * powerRZ 2 (c_qS c)) /\ ...
Theorem check_cert_correct : forall c,
  check_cert c = true -> Forall (point_sound c) (c_points c).
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Cells and coverings.}
\code{Deriv.v} differentiates expressions symbolically and proves the chain
rule for the result in CoqInterval's extended-real sense. A cell certificate
bounds a component over a rectangle of two varied slots (two angles, or an angle
and the radius) by the mean-value combination of its centre value and interval
enclosures of its derivatives over the rectangle (\code{check\_ccert\_correct}).
Reversed, the same data proves a component bounded away from zero, so that no
field of the reconstructed form balances in the cell. That the cells of a
certificate leave no gap is not assumed from the generator that wrote them:
\code{Cover.v} checks it as a chain of integer inequalities, so a certificate
claiming a range it does not cover is rejected like one whose bounds are too
small.

\paragraph{Harmonics and integrals.}
A spectral solution balances forces mode by mode over the modes it retains, so
the quantity it makes small is the discrete Fourier harmonic of the residual at
a retained mode, a finite sum $\sum_i r(p_i)\cos(mu_i-nv_i)$ over a uniform
grid of angles that \code{Project.v} encloses (\code{harm\_encloses}).
\code{TrigPoly.v} and \code{Harmonic.v} integrate exactly the components whose
trigonometric degree lies below the point counts of an equispaced grid, and
\code{Quad.v} encloses other angular integrals cell by cell. \code{Mercier.v}
assembles DShear, DCurr, DWell, DGeod and DMerc as VMEC's \code{mercier.f90}
defines them~\cite{mercier1960,bauer1984} from enclosed integrals, inside the
extracted code, so that a verdict is a theorem about the criterion rather than
about its ingredients. DGeod is the Cauchy--Schwarz defect of three surface
integrals and is proven never positive, whatever their enclosures
(\code{dgeod\_nonpositive}).

\paragraph{Boxes of states.}
In \code{Box.v} every input slot carries a half-width, so one interval
evaluation covers every field whose coefficients lie in a box. A floor certified
over a box cannot be an artefact of the one state at which a solver stopped.

\paragraph{Discrete equilibria.}
\code{Newton.v} proves that a passing interval Newton test in Krawczyk
form~\cite{krawczyk1969,moore2009} on any system of expressions gives exactly one
zero in its box: the map $x-AF(x)$ contracts the box into itself, an
approximate inverse of $A$ makes its fixed point a zero of $F$, and the
contraction makes that zero unique. \code{Colloc.v} collocates the residual of
\code{Physics.v} over a band of surfaces as such a system, with the surface
coefficients as unknowns (\code{colloc\_correct}). \code{Wide.v} evaluates the
system's outputs at the centre of the box in floating point of any precision.

\paragraph{Trust base.}
The kernel checks the proofs; what runs is the OCaml extraction of the checking
functions, linked with an unverified driver that parses certificates into the
extracted data types and prints verdicts. The trusted components are therefore
the Rocq kernel and extraction, the OCaml toolchain, the implementation of
Rocq's primitive 63-bit integers and binary64 floats, the driver's parser, and
the correspondence between a certificate's dyadic numbers and the doubles of
its wout file, which a separate tool, \code{gen/verify\_cert.py}, compares. For
the soundness theorems above, \code{Print Assumptions} reports the two axioms of
the standard library's classical Dedekind reals, excluded middle, functional
extensionality and the specifications of the primitive operations. The build
pins Rocq 9.1.1, CoqInterval 4.11.4, Flocq 4.2.2, Coquelicot 3.4.5 and OCaml
4.14.2.

\paragraph{Residuals and stability terms at low resolution.}
The certified residual of \code{li383\_low\_res} is insensitive to radial
refinement and falls with the mode set (Table~\ref{tab:results}): what a
converged VMEC++ run leaves at this resolution is the truncation of the mode
set, which the solver's convergence measure, taken over the retained modes, does
not see. At $s=0.125$ of \code{up\_down\_asym} with sixteen surfaces, the
certified current term of the Mercier criterion is $-3.7\times10^{-3}$ where the
file's, formed from difference quotients across surfaces, is
$-6.1\times10^{-4}$, and the difference reverses the sign of DMerc; re-solved at
33 to 257 surfaces the two fall toward zero together and the file's sign
survives. At coarse resolution the verdict on local stability
depends on how the radial derivatives are taken.

\paragraph{An orientation test that is not gauge invariant.}
Four invariance tests of VMEC++ have expected answers that the development
fixes: an axisymmetric input solved with and without toroidal modes
(every toroidal derivative of an axisymmetric reconstruction is the zero
expression), a stellarator-symmetric input solved through the symmetric and the
asymmetric code paths (a series whose coefficients vanish contributes nothing),
a restart with a shifted $(0,0)$ coefficient of $\lambda$ (\code{lambda\_gauge}),
and a five-period stellarator solved over one period and over the torus (the
reconstruction reads each mode by its absolute toroidal number). The
second exposed an orientation test that is not invariant under the poloidal
rotation VMEC applies to an asymmetric boundary: the test multiplies two
$m=1$ coefficients whose product the rotation can drive to zero, where the
invariant is the $m=1$ determinant $r^{cc}z^{sc}-r^{sc}z^{cc}$. Relabelling the
\code{up\_down\_asym\_current} boundary by $\theta\to\pi-\theta$ sends
educational\_VMEC to its iteration cap at $f_{sqr}=2.07\times10^{-2}$ instead of
converging to $5.07\times10^{-12}$. It was reported as
vmecpp\#788~\cite{issue788}, after which VMEC++ tests the
determinant~\cite{pr789}, and as educational\_VMEC\#27~\cite{issue27}.

\paragraph{Discrete equilibria and the order of the scheme.}
One, two and three surfaces of the Solov'ev equilibrium~\cite{soloviev1968}
each have exactly one zero of the collocated residual in a box shaped to the
coefficients, of radius $5.7\times10^{-10}$ to $6.3\times10^{-9}$, around a
centre that Newton's method moved $2.2\times10^{-6}$ to $3.5\times10^{-6}$
from the file's coefficients; with five surfaces the test does not pass.
\code{HalfGrid.v} proves the node rule second-order consistent with the
residual of the continuously reconstructed field (\code{node\_consistent}) and
derives the second-order bound that \code{discretization\_is\_consistent}
states from consistency and a stability hypothesis (\code{lax\_second\_order});
near the axis, where the innermost interval is read linearly, the rule is
proven first order in the radial derivatives. Manufactured
solutions~\cite{roache2002} measure the order directly. A manufactured mapping
on the annulus $1/4<s<1$ is not an equilibrium; its continuum residual at the
collocation points is a source $f$, and the zero $x_h$ of $r-f$ over the
interior nodes, with $\lambda$ given so that the problem sits on the quotient by
the poloidal gauge, is the discrete solution. The interval Newton test puts
exactly one zero in a box $5\times10^{-18}$ wide per unknown around a centre
refined on outputs read at 128 bits, so the error $\max|x_h-x^*|$ is known to
twelve digits (Table~\ref{tab:results}). It falls by 3.81, 3.92 and 3.97 per
doubling for a three-dimensional mapping and by 3.73 and 3.87 for one without
stellarator symmetry (Figure~\ref{fig:conv}a), and it is unchanged at five times
the pressure, which enters the radial force only as a flux function the rule and
its source share.

\paragraph{A forced current sheet.}
On VMEC++'s circular tokamak ($R_0=6$, $a=2$) with $\iota=0.9-0.64s$, which is
$1/2$ at $s=0.625$, and a $(1,1)$ boundary ripple of amplitude $0.03$, toroidal
coupling drives a $(2,1)$ harmonic of the radial force on the rational surface.
Its certified value, the rectangle rule for the integral of $r_s\cos(2u-v)$ on
a $64\times32$ grid of angles, enclosed by \code{dharm\_correct}, stays between
$6.2\times10^{-5}$ and $9.1\times10^{-5}$ from 65 to 257 surfaces and is linear
in the ripple; the enclosure at 65 surfaces holds for every state within a
relative $10^{-12}$ of the converged coefficients. On the same surface the
axisymmetric harmonics $(2,0)$ and $(1,0)$ fall by 3.8 to 5.3 per doubling, the
order of the scheme, while the $(3,1)$ sideband falls by only 1.2 to 1.3
(Figure~\ref{fig:conv}b), and
the same ripple on a transform profile with no low-order rational surface nearby
leaves nothing behind. This is the obstruction a nested-surface field meets at a
rational surface~\cite{grad1967,hahm1985}, whose ideal response is a current
sheet. In SPEC~\cite{hudson2012}, two Beltrami volumes separated by an ideal
interface at the rational surface carry the $(2,1)$ sheet as a jump of
$1.47\times10^{-5}$ in the covariant poloidal field at ripple $0.03$, as in the
equilibria of~\cite{loizu2015}, and with the rational surface inside a relaxed
volume SPEC's field-line tracing finds a $(2,1)$ island whose width grows from
$0.107$ to $0.170$ in the volume's radial coordinate as the ripple goes from
$0.0075$ to $0.03$.

\paragraph{Free-boundary balance.}
At a free-boundary equilibrium $p+B^2/2$ is continuous across the plasma
boundary, where outside it is the pressure of the full vacuum field. VMEC++
computes that field with NESTOR~\cite{merkel1986,hirshman1986}; BIEST's
virtual-casing field~\cite{malhotra2019,malhotra2020} gives it independently. The
certificate sweeps the vacuum pressure over the segment between the two answers,
$P_\mathrm{vac}=P_\mathrm{mid}+t\,\delta P$ with $t\in[-1,1]$, and the plasma
side is VMEC's extrapolation of the two outermost half points to $s=1$. On the
CTH-like test case, NESTOR's jump where it is largest is certified at least
$7.556\times10^{-4}$ at 25 surfaces, and the jump at every point of NESTOR's
grid at most $7.560\times10^{-4}$ for every $t$. At 49, 97, 193 and 385
surfaces each such ceiling lies below the floor of the resolution before it,
down to $[4.9648,4.9672]\times10^{-4}$ at 385, so the certified jump falls under
radial refinement. Covered over the whole boundary by $512\times128$ cells per field
period, with the vacuum side the trigonometric interpolant of each answer's
grid, it is at most $8.5\times10^{-3}$ at 25 surfaces and $1.05\times10^{-2}$
at 385: between grid points the bound is set by that interpolant, whose angular
resolution radial refinement leaves as it is.

\paragraph{Quasisymmetry.}
A field is quasisymmetric exactly when the two-term ratio is a flux
function~\cite{helander2008,helander2014}. For a quasisymmetric field every discrete harmonic
of the defect of the claim ``the ratio equals $F_0$'' is one number times the
matching harmonic of its second term, so two harmonic ratios with disjoint
enclosures refute it (\code{two\_term\_refuted}). On \code{wout\_nfp4\_QH\_ns50}
the certified ratios span 1.7 at mid radius, where the axisymmetric Solov'ev
equilibrium, quasisymmetric by construction, spans $2\times10^{-4}$ from the
reconstruction's own residual; the span is unchanged from 50 to 162 modes. The
field of that file is not quasisymmetric on any surface tested.

\paragraph{Use in VMEC++.}
The certified values enter a branch of VMEC++~\cite{branch} as regression tests
whose tolerances are the enclosures: the manufactured-solution errors, the
resonant floor with the fall of the axisymmetric harmonics, and the
free-boundary jump, which reads NESTOR's boundary vacuum field through an option
proposed upstream~\cite{pr903}. Tests that follow from theorems here are part
of VMEC++: a finite difference of its energy equals its unpreconditioned forces
contracted with the perturbation, the discrete form of
\code{energy\_gradient\_is\_force}~\cite{pr905}, and three of the invariances
above hold~\cite{pr875}. Tests that DGeod is never positive and that the energy
Hessian is symmetric are proposed~\cite{pr904}.

\section{Related work}

Machine-checked numerical analysis has covered the discretization and the
floating-point program of the one-dimensional wave equation~\cite{boldo2013},
verified ODE enclosures up to the Lorenz attractor~\cite{immler2018}, and the
checking of large certificates in the formal proof of the Kepler
conjecture~\cite{hales2017}. Computer-assisted proofs for partial differential
equations are well developed outside proof assistants~\cite{nakao2019}. In
plasma physics, equilibrium codes are verified by convergence studies,
manufactured solutions and comparison between codes such as VMEC and
SPEC~\cite{hudson2012,loizu2015}. We know of no prior work that states the
output of a stellarator equilibrium code as a machine-checked theorem about the
field it defines.

\section{Conclusion}

Stellarocq gives the output of an equilibrium code a meaning a proof assistant
can check: bounds, harmonics, stability terms, quasisymmetry verdicts and the
existence of discrete solutions, each a theorem about the field reconstructed
from the file's exact numbers. The same machinery extends from the equilibrium
to its coil field, where it carries a machine-checked KAM argument for the flux
surfaces of a real stellarator, the subject of separate work.
